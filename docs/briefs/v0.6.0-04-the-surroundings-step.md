## Goal

`pipeline/mattini/surroundings.py` turns swissALTIRegio's 10 m tiles into the height grid of
the far terrain around the area, which brief v0.6.0-08 draws as a mesh (design §3):

- **The grid:** a vertex every 50 m over the area's box grown by 20 km (841 × 861 for the
  village).
- **Its heights:** each vertex is the MEAN of the 25 samples of its square, computed exactly in
  whole centimetres, so the result does not depend on the order of summation (design §1).
- **The cut:** vertices strictly inside the area are NaN; the real terrain is there.

The file is compared with design's expected SHA-256 before it is written, as the terrain's is.
The command line and the manifest come in brief v0.6.0-07: this brief builds the step and the
check the manifest will call, not their wiring.

## Decisions already made

Decisions 1 to 10 are brief M4-02's (design, 2026-10-17), carried over; "changed" marks what

1. **The rule** (design §1, amending M4-02 decision 1):
   - **The box** (M4-02): the area box grown by `margin_m` on every side. The area's corners and
     `margin_m` must be multiples of `spacing_m`; otherwise the step refuses, naming the world
     config, the area and the spacing.
   - **The grid** (M4-02; spacing changed to 50): row r, column c is LV95 (e_min + c·s,
     n_max − r·s), edges included. Rows run north to south, columns west to east. The village:
     E 2'603'000–2'645'000, N 1'075'000–1'118'000, **841 columns × 861 rows**.
   - **The height (changed):** the **mean** of the samples whose position lies in the half-open
     square [E − s/2, E + s/2) × [N − s/2, N + s/2), minus `height_offset_m`:
     - every height text is parsed to **whole centimetres** (`-?[0-9]+` with at most two
       decimals after a point: `2430.6` is 243060, `2431` is 243100, `-3.05` is −305, `2430.10`
       is 243010);
     - the n samples' centimetres are **summed as integers**, divided **once** in float64
       (`sum_cm / (100 · n)`), the float64 `height_offset_m` subtracted, and the result
       **rounded once** to float32. No summation order can change it. The offset is
       subtracted in float64 after the exact quotient; for an offset that is a whole number of
       centimetres times a power of two (the village's 0.0, the fixture's 1250.25) this equals
       the exact-rational rule the fixture's oracle uses; an offset such as 0.1 is covered by no
       pin.
     - n is the number of samples the square holds: 25 at the village's 10 m samples and 50 m
       spacing (5 × 5, at V − 25, V − 15, V − 5, V + 5, V + 15 per axis: the samples on the
       square's west and south edges are its own, those on its east and north edges are the
       neighbour's). A square lacking some samples (a tile at the data's edge) takes the mean of
       those it holds (the session's reading of design §1's "n"; pinned).
     - Why the mean (design §1): a drawn surface of maxima bulges up to 418 m above the ground;
       the 50 m mean loses 15 to 31 m at the summits.
   - **A height text that is not whole centimetres (changed)** — three decimals, an exponent, a
     `+`, a bare point, `nan`, `inf`, an underscore, a comma — is refused naming the tile, the
     line (`line <k>`, the member's header being line 1), the text and `python -m mattini
     download regio` (design §1; M4-01 decision 3: at most two decimals).
   - **The cut** (M4-02): a vertex strictly inside the area box (e_min < E < e_max and
     n_min < N < n_max) is NaN, the quiet NaN **0x7FC00000** (bytes `00 00 c0 7f`). Vertices on
     the area's edge keep their height. The village: 2301 NaN (39 × 59).
   - **A vertex with no sample** (M4-02) is refused, naming its row, column, position and
     `python -m mattini download regio`, if it keeps a height. A vertex inside the cut is NaN
     whatever its square holds.
