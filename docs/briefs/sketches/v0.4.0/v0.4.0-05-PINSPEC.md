The rule's reading. Godot's shader multiplies the horizon's light by `ground_color` and then raises
the sum to 1/(1.2 + 1.2 · sun_fade), 1/2.4 by day; the uniform is `source_color`, so Forward+ linearises
the value first. A value G renders the sky's ground at (G + 0.055) / 1.055 of the horizon's light (for G
above 0.04): at K itself (1.133, 1.166, 0.769) that is (1.126, 1.157, 0.781) against ρK, 1 to 2 %; the
numpy shader's ground at K (0.1158, 0.1307, 0.1002) against ρ × E/π (0.1170, 0.1324, 0.0966). So the
literal reading (the value set is ρ × K) is taken; the exact-radiance alternative, (ρK)^2.4 linearised
back, differs by under 2 % at K and was not taken.
