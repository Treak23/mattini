## What each pinned file pins, and why

**`test_m4_04_sky_clock_vectors.gd`** (6 tests): SkyClock agrees with design's vectors, computed by
other methods than the clock's (pvlib's SPA for the sun with NOAA's refraction added to SPA's true
elevation; skyfield with DE421 for the moon and the sidereal time; Python's zoneinfo for the offset
and the text), 295 instants from 2020 to 2035 at 46.02 N, 7.749 E, and 12 at Cape Town, Reykjavik and
Tokyo. Tolerances: sun 0.05° above 2° expected elevation, 0.3° at or below; moon 0.5° in azimuth ×
cos(elevation) and in elevation, fraction 0.03; sidereal 0.01°; offset and text exact. The other
sites kill a site written into the clock and latitude and longitude swapped (southern hemisphere,
west of Greenwich, far east). `test_the_vectors_are_designs` reads only the data (the site, 150 to
300 instants, one start): it passes without SkyClock's values, by design.

**`test_m4_04_sky_clock_rules.gd`** (6 tests): exact rules and the likeliest wrong twins by name
(lesson 34): START_UNIX 1694000400 with its text and offset; the 32 switch instants 2020 to 2035
written out (not computed in the test), each with a second and a millisecond before, the instant
itself, 00:30 UTC (the twin "switch at local 01:00"), mid-winter and mid-summer; twelve texts
exactly (switch edges, the year's end, 29 Feb 2024, minutes truncated at 13:40:59.9, 2035); the
azimuth true and clockwise at the start (184.886), a June morning (68.153: the mirror gives 291.8,
the south's count 248.2) and evening (296.619); UTC not local (the start's sun and LMST 168.06001);
the sidereal time in degrees (LMST, GMST 160.31101, six hours on: + 90.246°).

Every value in the pins was re-derived a second way by this rehearsal (§Two ways, below).
