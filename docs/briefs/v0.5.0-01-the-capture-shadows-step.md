## Goal

`pipeline/mattini/shadows.py` computes where the photo (SWISSIMAGE 2023) lay in shadow when it
was flown, from the swissSURFACE3D 0.5 m tiles that `download surface` already fetches (for the
trees), and writes it as a 0.5 m mask: `shadows/<area>.png` (0 lit, 255 shadow), its
`.png.import` and its description `shadows/<area>.json`. A later batch of this release lifts the
photo inside the mask in the terrain shader (M4-06's lift), so that the moving sun's shadows read
and the photo's do not darken a second time (SPEC §13 row 41; kept with G = 3, row 47).

- **The rule:** cast shadow by a march towards the flight's sun on 1 m cells, own shadow from
  the cells' normals; then each 1 m cell's shadow on its 2 × 2 cells of 0.5 m, and an inset at
  0.5 m that keeps a shadow cell only inside the shadow (half a metre off every edge, as the
  prototype the owner judged).
- **The output** is compared with design's expected mask and PNG bytes before anything is
  written, as the surfaces step does (lesson 38, brain.md H27).
- The command line (`python -m mattini shadows`) and the manifest's `shadows` block come in
  brief v0.5.0-02 (M4-09's shadows part), which calls this brief's `build_shadows` and
  `built_shadows_block`. Nothing in the game, the manifest or `__main__.py` changes here.

1. **The rule** is m4-design §3, made exact here (the pinned `test_m4_03_rule.py` writes it out
   cell by cell; its docstring is the reference):
   - **The cells:** 1 m, aligned to the area's north-west corner, rows north to south, columns
     west to east; cell (r, c) has its centre at E = e_min + 0.5 + c, N = n_max − 0.5 − r. Its
     height h is the largest of its 2 × 2 swissSURFACE3D pixels, in float64. The heights are
     the tiles' LV95 heights (EPSG:5728); `height_offset_m` plays no part (only differences
     count).
   - **Cast shadow:** with A the grid azimuth and e the elevation, the offset towards the sun is
     computed once per k = 1 … K, in cells: dx = k·sin A (east), dy = −(k·cos A) (south);
     i0 = floor(dx), fx = dx − i0, j0 = floor(dy), fy = dy − j0. The surface there is bilinear
     in the four cells (r + j0 [+1], c + i0 [+1]), evaluated as north = h00·(1 − fx) + h01·fx,
     south = h10·(1 − fx) + h11·fx, s = north·(1 − fy) + south·fy. A column or row whose weight
     is 0 is not needed. **The grid is the cells' centres:** a point whose needed cells are not
     all inside it casts nothing (nothing is clamped or padded). The cell is in cast shadow if
     s > (h + k·tan e) + threshold_m for any k. The offsets per k, not per point, so that a
     vectorised shift and a per-cell loop give the same bits.
   - **K** = ceil((max − min) / tan e) over the area's cells, in float64. The last step can
     never cast (a hit needs k < (max − min − threshold) / tan e), so `range(1, K)` is an
     equivalent mutant; `march_steps` is pinned to the definition anyway.
   - **Own shadow:** gx = dh/dE and gy = dh/dN by central differences over the two neighbours
     divided by 2, one-sided and not halved on the grid's edges; with (ux, uy, uz) = (cos e·sin A,
     cos e·cos A, sin e), the cell is in its own shadow when ((−gx)·ux − gy·uy) + uz ≤ 0.
   - **Ties:** strict `>` for the cast, `≤ 0` for the normal, as m4-design §3 writes them.
   - **The mask at 0.5 m** (design's ruling of 2026-10-17, amending m4-design §3, which wrote
     the inset at 1 m): the march's result, cast or own shadow per 1 m cell, becomes the 2 × 2
     cells of 0.5 m of each 1 m cell (rows 2r and 2r + 1, columns 2c and 2c + 1). Then the
     inset runs on the 0.5 m grid: a 0.5 m cell in shadow is 255 only when its four neighbours
     inside the grid are in shadow too (a neighbour outside the grid does not count); every
     other cell 0. The mask has twice the 1 m grid's rows and columns; its cell (r, c) has its
     centre at E = e_min + 0.25 + 0.5·c, N = n_max − 0.25 − 0.5·r. Why: an inset at 1 m eroded
     a whole metre (32 % raw shadow became 20 % kept); the owner judged the prototype's 0.5 m
     erosion (`docs/briefs/sketches/M4-soften/README.md`).
2. **The sun** (`config/shadows.json`, design data): grid azimuth **184.67°**, elevation 50.1°,
   threshold 0.05 m, the march on 1 m cells, the mask in 0.5 m cells. The flight's mean true
   azimuth is 184.9°; at the village LV95 grid north lies 0.226° east of true north, so the grid
   azimuth is the true one **minus** 0.226° (m4-design §3's 185.13° had the sign reversed;
   design's rehearsal of 2026-10-17). `config/sky.json` holds `true_to_grid_deg` −0.2258
   (added to a true azimuth it gives the grid one); the pinned `test_m4_03_village_config.py`
   ties the two configs together: round(184.9 + (−0.2258), 2) = 184.67.
3. **The API** (the signatures are the API; the pins import them, and brief v0.5.0-02 calls
   `build_shadows` and `built_shadows_block`):

     elevation_deg)`, `cast_shadow(heights, azimuth_grid_deg, elevation_deg, threshold_m)`,
     `own_shadow(heights, azimuth_grid_deg, elevation_deg)` (bool 1 m grids), `inset(shadow)`
     (any bool grid → uint8 0/255, the same shape), `capture_mask(surface, azimuth_grid_deg,

4. **The inputs:** the `swisssurface3d` lock entry and `<raw>/swisssurface3d/2021-2022/` with

   the config; the world (the owner's axes only, the area, `computed_for.world_sha256`); the
   lock's fit (`lock.fit_problem`, then `shape_problem`, naming `python -m mattini lock
