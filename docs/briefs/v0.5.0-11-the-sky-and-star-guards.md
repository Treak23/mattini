placeholder sun equal to `config/shadows.json`'s flight sun (S-02); `SkySite` stands in the

guards, tests and words.

## Decisions already made

"(pin author; accepted by the session 2026-10-30)" marks a choice the pin author made while
rehearsing. Each item's source is the go's question A and its review item.

1. **S-02, test only.** `main.gd`'s `FALLBACK_SUN_AZIMUTH_GRID_DEG` (184.67) and
   `FALLBACK_SUN_ELEVATION_DEG` (50.1) equal `config/shadows.json`'s `sun.azimuth_grid_deg` and
   `sun.elevation_deg`, compared as floats from the script's constant map and the file's JSON. No
   change to `main.gd`: its comment already says the two are a hand copy and that nothing reads
   the file there, which stays true.
2. **S-03, test only** (pin author; accepted by the session 2026-10-30). `SkySite` joins
   `READERS` and `BLOCKS` (`"sky"`) in `test_m3_r1_fix5_type_guards.gd`, so every every-reader case
   (the top-level and the block's `world_sha256` of the wrong type, an origin or height offset of
   the wrong type) reads it too; a new case refuses each of its own fields (`area`,
   `latitude_deg`, `longitude_deg`, `true_to_grid_deg`) of the wrong type or value with its own
   words. `test_m3_r1_fix5_world_guard.gd` gives it the three moved worlds (east 1000 m, north
   37.5 m, height 0.25 m) on the shared shadows fixture's manifest, the one shared fixture with a sky
   block. `SkySite`'s words for another world config are its own ("the sun's place was written for
   another world config", where the six say "built"): the census reads each reader's needle; no
   change to `sky_site.gd` (the triage's "do not change sky_site.gd unless a case fails as a real
   bug": none did).
3. **S-05, the guard.** `SkyRig.star_image` refuses, after a line's three numbers parse and before
   anything is drawn: a right ascension not in [0, 360) degrees, then a declination not strictly
   between -90 and 90. Each refusal is one `push_error` naming the file, `line <n>:` and the words
   `right ascension` or `declination`, and a null result, as the "not three numbers" refusal is.
   Write each check so that NaN and the infinities fail it (`not (ra >= 0.0 and ra < 360.0)`,
   `not (dec > -90.0 and dec < 90.0)`): `"1e400".is_valid_float()` is true and reads as inf
   (rehearsal; `"nan"` and `"inf"` are not valid floats). Why: at a declination of exactly ±90 the
   run `floor(0.5 / cos(dec))` is about 8.2e15 texels and the loop never returns (rehearsal: a
   probe hung until killed); beyond 90 it is negative and the star vanishes without a word. The
   star image's doc comment says so. The words of each message beyond those needles are yours.
   The right ascension guard is the pin author's extension of S-05 (its residue text asks only for
   the |dec| >= 90 guard; a right ascension out of range is drawn at a wrong column without a
   word), accepted by the session 2026-10-30.
4. **S-05, the bytes.** Inside the range nothing changes: the image of `res://sky/stars.csv` keeps
   its bytes, SHA-256 of `Image.get_data()`
   `37dcea6f33dd492452bcc2913e1ab4c83e9bf806ddc00560f10e1d85a1faa3c6` (33554432 bytes, 7204 texels
   lit), measured two ways (GDScript's HashingContext, and a Python re-implementation with Godot's
   RGBE9995 packing: the same hash).
5. **S-05, `tools/stars_table.py`, test only.** No change to the script: its `_check` and `build`
   are separable already. The pin runs it on a 17-byte file (exit 1, the size line, no output),
   calls `_check` on 53316318 zero bytes (the SHA-256 line), and runs `build` on eight synthetic
   records (no catalogue is downloaded) whose expected lines were computed by hand; two share a V
   and two share a V and a right ascension, each pair out of order in the file, so the documented
   tie-breaks (right ascension, then declination) are pinned (the checker's P3, re-cut).
6. **CD-3.** The `night_sky` declaration gains `repeat_enable` after Godot's three hints:
   `uniform sampler2D night_sky : filter_linear, source_color, hint_default_black, repeat_enable;`
   (the exact line: `test_m4_05_sky_rig.gd`'s `GODOT_LINES` holds it, re-pinned for this one line).
   Directly above it, `// mattini:` comment lines (the shader's rule: every change marked on a line
   of its own) name `repeat_enable`, the seam at right ascension 0/360 it removes, and say that the
   wrap of v across the poles is harmless (rows 0 and 2047 hold no star: the table's stars lie on
   rows 8 to 2036, rehearsal). The pin reads the words `repeat_enable`, `360` and `pole` there.
   This is the one item the owner can see: it changes the night sky's sampling by one texel at the
   seam (and blends rows 0 and 2047 within half a texel of a pole, both black), which the owner
   admitted by naming CD-3 in the go's list (beyond "tests and messages").
7. **png_l8's words.** `png_l8(pixels, top=ROAD, *, what="classes")`: a keyword-only argument whose
   default keeps the classes' three messages byte for byte; the three refusals read "the <what>
   must be a two-dimensional array of uint8", "<columns> × <rows> <what> do not make a PNG" and
   "the <what> are 0 to <top>, found <max>". Both flow encodings (in `write_surfaces` when no
   `flow_png` is given, and the step's) pass `what="flow codes"`; `write_surfaces`'s docstring
   names the call as it now is. Both classes' encodings (in `write_surfaces` when no `png` is
   given, and the step's) keep the default words: the pin refuses a class grid holding a 5 in each
   with exactly "the classes are 0 to 4, found 5" (the checker's P3, re-cut: the likeliest wrong
   twin, a classes' call given the flow's words). No byte of any PNG changes: the designed fixture PNGs' SHA-256s
   stay pinned by `test_surfaces_fixture.py` and `test_surfaces_flow.py`, and the pin writes both
   PNGs again with the keyword. `test_v040_01_flow_gaps.py` matches "0 to 240, found 241" and
   stays green.
8. **TG-09, test only.** `test_v040_04_sky_clock_margins.gd` (the v0.4.0-04 fixer's file, now

9. **AZ-360, words only.** `SkyClock.sun`'s and `SkyClock.moon`'s doc comments say the azimuth is

   `360.0` and `north` in each, and holds the engine fact itself (a double 1e-6 below 360 is 360.0
   in a Vector2's and a Vector3's x). No code change: every consumer takes the azimuth's sine and
   cosine (`main.gd`'s `_direction`), where 360 and 0 agree.
