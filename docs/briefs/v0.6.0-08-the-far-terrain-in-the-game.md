## Goal

The game can read and build the far terrain around the area (design §3):

- **The reader**, `SurroundingsGrid` (`game/scripts/terrain/surroundings_grid.gd`), reads the
  manifest's `surroundings` block, its 50 m heights (`surroundings/<area>.f32`) and its 10 m colour
  (`surroundings/<area>_colour.jpg`), refuses every problem with one error naming the manifest
  (Hard Rule 2), and places the grid through the world config.
- **The loader**, `SurroundingsLoader` (`surroundings_loader.gd`), builds the drawn mesh: chunks of
  80 × 80 squares (4 km at 50 m; 121 for the village), smooth normals, the colour over the whole
  box, one material for the chunks, and a double-sided skirt with its own material that hangs
  100 m down along the edge of the cut, where the near terrain begins. The far mesh casts
  shadows; the skirt does not.

Nothing calls them yet: the main scene wires them in brief v0.6.0-09 (the far 40 km camera, the
startup line, the error panel's entry, the credits). The owner sees nothing new in this batch.

5. **The vertex** (M4-07 decisions 2 and 5): column c, row r at `Vector3(x_min + c·s, height,
   z_min + r·s)`: `x_min` and `z_min` come from `world.to_local` (narrowed there, H5), plus c·s
   and r·s in GDScript's 64-bit float, narrowed once more (no test can tell this from a 32-bit
   sum below 1 mm: the village's origin is a multiple of 50 m); y exactly the grid's height (the
   file holds local heights, h − height_offset_m: v0.6.0-04 decision 1).
   **The trap world** (pinned): the loader's test writes the fixture with its origin moved to E
   2'618'250.13 / N 1'101'750.44, which float32 holds only as 2'618'250.25 / 1'101'750.5, so a
   32-bit subtraction puts every vertex 0.12 m west and 0.06 m south; the tolerance is 1 mm.
6. **The normal** (design §3; the formula the pin author's): (−dh/dx, 1, −dh/dz) normalised, with
   dh/dx along +x (east, column + 1) and dh/dz along +z (south, row + 1) by central differences of
   the grid, (h[c+1] − h[c−1]) / 2s; one-sided, (h[c+1] − h[c]) / s or (h[c] − h[c−1]) / s, where
   the neighbour lies outside the box or is NaN; computed on the whole grid, so a vertex shared by
   two chunks has one normal (design §0's probe: no seams). Checked within 0.001 per component
   (Godot stores normals octahedral in 16 bits: rehearsal, the read-back differs by about 1e-4).
7. **The UV** (design §3): (c / (columns − 1), r / (rows − 1)), which is ((E − e_min) / (e_max −
   e_min), (n_max − N) / (n_max − n_min)): the box's edges are the texture's edges, u east, v south,
   (0, 0) at the north-west corner. Within 1e-6.
8. **The skirt** (design §3; **double-sided by the session's ruling of 2026-11-02**): one
   `MeshInstance3D` "Skirt", `cast_shadow` `SHADOW_CASTING_SETTING_OFF`. An edge segment is a grid
   edge between a drawn square (four finite corners) and an undrawn one, both inside the box; the
   box's own outer edge has none. For each, a vertical quad of two triangles from the segment's two
   vertices down **100 m**, each quad with its own four vertices, their normals horizontal across
   the segment (which way is yours), their UVs those of the grid vertex above them. It is drawn
   with its own material, both faces (decision 9), so which way its front faces is not pinned:
   seen from inside the cut it covers the crack where the far edge lies above the near terrain
   (design §3's job; a skirt facing out of the cut and culled would be seen from nowhere the crack
   is), seen from outside it lies under the far surface's own edge. The fixture's ring: 14
   segments; the village's: 200 (rehearsal, 400 triangles: design §0 measured the same).
9. **The material** (design §3, amended by the session's ruling of 2026-11-02: the mesh's chunks
