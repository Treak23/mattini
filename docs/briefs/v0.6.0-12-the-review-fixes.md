## Decisions already made

1. **The camera's floor (A1; P1).** The far terrain is ground to the camera wherever it is drawn. `SurroundingsGrid` gains
   `height_at(x: float, z: float) -> float`: the local height of the drawn surface under local (x, z), computed on the loader's own
   triangles, and NaN where nothing is drawn (outside the box, in the cut, or in a square with a NaN corner). The loader's layout
   (its header): with a the north-west, b the north-east, c the south-west and d the south-east corner of a square, the triangles
   are (a, b, c) and (b, d, c), so the diagonal runs from b to c. With u in [0, 1] eastward and v in [0, 1] southward inside the
   square: where u + v <= 1, h = ha + u (hb − ha) + v (hc − ha); where u + v >= 1, h = hd + (1 − u)(hc − hd) + (1 − v)(hb − hd).
   x and z are local coordinates (`x = E − origin_e`, `z = −(N − origin_n)`): the square is found from the grid's own `x_min`,
   `z_min` and `spacing_m`, which the reader took from the WorldConfig; no LV95 number and no origin appears in the new code (Hard
   Rule 4), and the heights are already local (the loader's y). A point on a square's shared edge belongs to a drawn square if
   either neighbour is drawn; the last row and column's far edge belongs to the last square. The convention lives in one place both
   the loader and `height_at` use (a constant or a static function: your shape), so the surface the camera rests on and the surface
   drawn cannot drift apart.
2. **`main.gd`.** The main scene keeps the far grid it read. `_ground_height(x, z)` returns the near terrain's height where it is
   finite, else the far terrain's, else NaN, and is null-safe for both (the far terrain is read whatever else is refused: with the
   terrain refused the far floor still holds, and with no far terrain the behaviour is today's). `_camera.height_at` is set when
   either is built, not only in `_show_terrain`. `FreeFlyCamera` is unchanged (its clearance stays `clearance_m`, 1.5 m; the
   "free where there is no ground" case stays for the places with neither).
3. **The seam.** At the area box's edge the floor passes from the near terrain's to the far terrain's, which may stand metres higher
   (a 50 m mean over the 0.5 m ground, in gullies and on convex ridges the other way); the camera is lifted at the step and not
   lowered again, because `_keep_above_ground` only raises. Accepted; recorded in Findings. No smoothing.
4. **Tests (yours; the harms they must catch).** A new file `game/tests/test_v060_12_camera_floor.gd`, on an asymmetric far grid
   (heights rising to the south-east and differing on the two diagonals' sides, lesson 34, so a mirrored or swapped axis, a
   flipped diagonal and a north-up z all fail): `height_at` equals the height of the loader's own mesh at sample points (read the
   triangles from the `ArrayMesh` arrays of the built `Surroundings` node: both triangles of a square, points on the diagonal, on
   the box's edges and corners, a point with a fractional LV95-derived coordinate far from the origin, H5); NaN in the cut, outside
   the box and beside a NaN corner; the main scene: a camera put under the far surface outside the box by `set_view` is lifted to
   the surface plus the clearance; with the terrain refused the far floor still holds; with the far block absent nothing changes;
   inside the area box the near terrain's height still governs. Plus one optional gaps file `game/tests/test_v060_12_gaps.gd`
   (lesson 51) for the prover's findings.
5. **Words (Hard Rule 2; A4, A6, A7).** `main.gd`'s header says the far plane reaches every corner of the far terrain "from anywhere
   in the area box" and that the camera's floor is the near terrain's inside it and the far terrain's outside. `surroundings.py`
   `_lock_problem`'s docstring and `farcolour.py` `_checked`'s comment say what is true: `fit_problem` raises `DownloadError` only
   for a datasets config the earlier checks (`_regio_config`, `_far_config`) have already refused, so the `except` is unreachable
   today and is kept so that a future change that lets one through ends as a refusal and not a traceback; the handlers stay (their
   stand-in tests stay). `FAR_COLOUR_NOTE`'s "100 m" stays a literal in the game, and a new pytest census (your new file
   `pipeline/tests/test_v060_12_notes.py`) reads `game/scripts/main.gd` and `config/surroundings.json` and requires the number in the
   note to equal `colour.band_m` (a census proves text: it says so).
6. **The Release notes (A9, A10; lesson 2).** `tools/release_notes.md` gains, after the Credits block's software paragraph or in a
   section of its own: (a) the placeholders the pause screen shows, word for word for the fixed ones (`BuildingLoader.WALLS_NOTE`,
