## Goal

The scene's light can follow the sun and the moon wherever they stand, under a physical sky whose
ground below the horizon is the valley's own colour.

- **`SkyRig`** (`game/scripts/sky/sky_rig.gd`, new) takes the directions towards the sun and the
  moon as local unit vectors and sets the sun light, a moon light, the exposure and the sky.
- **The sky** is Godot 4.6.3's physical sky converted to a sky shader
  (`game/shaders/sky_physical.gdshader`, new), with a twilight towards the sun from +6° to −12°, a
  faint glow of the night sky, the sun's disk, and a night sky that fades in after dusk.
- **The sky's ground** (SPEC §13 row 52): the rig sets the shader's `ground_color` to a measured
  constant, `SkyRig.GROUND_COLOUR`, so a shaded wall or tree side keeps at least 0.3.1's light
  relative to lit flat ground.

The rig is built and tested on its own nodes. Nothing the owner sees changes in this batch: the
main scene uses the rig from v0.4.0-08, which converts the clock's angles into local vectors through
WorldConfig. The stars (the star table, the star image, the celestial basis) are v0.4.0-06's.

1. **The rig** (M4-05 decision 1, without section S). `class_name SkyRig`, `extends RefCounted`.
   - `static func create(sun: DirectionalLight3D, moon: DirectionalLight3D, environment:
     Environment, frame: Basis, latitude_deg: float, stars: Image = null) -> SkyRig`. `frame`'s
     columns are the scene's local east, north and up (main: `Basis(_east, _north, _up)`). It sets
     what never changes: the sun's shadows on, `directional_shadow_max_distance` 3000,
     `light_angular_distance` 0 (sharp shadows, as today; design's ruling of 2026-10-17, for the
     reason measured in decision 4c: the sky draws the sun's disk from its own uniform); the moon's
     shadows on, `sky_mode` `SKY_MODE_LIGHT_ONLY` (so the sun stays the sky's `LIGHT0`), colour
     (0.80, 0.85, 1.0); the environment's background the sky, a `Sky` whose material is a
     `ShaderMaterial` of `res://shaders/sky_physical.gdshader` (property `sky_material` of the rig),
     ambient light from the sky with contribution 0.25, Filmic; **the material's `ground_color` set
     to `GROUND_COLOUR` (Decision 6)**. With `stars`, an `ImageTexture` of it is the material's
     `night_sky` (keep the parameter: v0.4.0-06 passes the star image through it). `latitude_deg` is
     kept on the rig for v0.4.0-06's celestial basis; nothing reads it in this brief (say so in a
     comment).
   - `func apply(to_sun: Vector3, to_moon: Vector3, moon_fraction: float, sidereal_deg: float) ->
     void`, every frame. Each elevation is `asin(direction · up)` of the frame's up, never the
     scene's y. Each light's basis is `Basis.looking_at(-direction, up)` (it shines along its −z, as
     M3's sun). It sets the sky material's `stars_strength`, `sun_direction` (`to_sun` normalised,
     every frame, also while the sun light is hidden) and `twilight_colour` (decision 2). It does not
     touch `ground_color`. `sidereal_deg` is unused until v0.4.0-06 (name it `_sidereal_deg` so gdlint
     accepts it).
2. **The curves** (M4-05 decision 2; m4-design §6), all against the elevation in degrees:
   - sun energy `smoothstep(-1, 10, e)`; hidden below −1; colour white at 10 and above, mixed
     linearly to (1.0, 0.72, 0.50) at 0, kept below 0;
   - moon energy `0.03 × fraction × smoothstep(0, 5, e)`; hidden below 0;
   - exposure 0.70 at a sun elevation of 10 and above, 4.0 at −12 and below,
     `exp(lerp(ln 0.70, ln 4.0, (10 − e) / 22))` between;

     in GDScript and passed as a uniform, so the pinned test reads the real function: 0.5 × sunset at
     +3°, (0.675, 0.425, 0.425) at −3°, 0.84375 × (0.35, 0.30, 0.55) at −6°, 0 at +6° and −12°.

4. **The sky shader** (M4-05 decision 4, without section S) is Godot's generated code with
   `p_use_debanding` and `p_use_night_sky` true, its `%s` filled as Godot fills them (`render_mode
   use_debanding;`, `L0 += texture(night_sky, SKY_COORDS).xyz * extinction;`, `COLOR =
   texture(night_sky, SKY_COORDS).xyz;`), every other line kept, and these changes only, each marked
   `// mattini:` in a comment on its own line (the census of Godot's lines compares whole lines, so a
   comment on the same line as code breaks it):
   - one uniform after `night_sky`: `uniform float stars_strength : hint_range(0, 1) = 0.0;`
     (v0.4.0-06 adds `uniform mat3 celestial_basis` after it; not you);
   - both night-sky lines multiply by `stars_strength`: `L0 += texture(night_sky,
     SKY_COORDS).xyz * extinction * stars_strength;` and `COLOR = texture(night_sky,
     SKY_COORDS).xyz * stars_strength;` (they keep `SKY_COORDS`; v0.4.0-06 changes the coordinates);
   - twilight and glow (design's rulings of 2026-10-17), four uniforms declared exactly `uniform vec3
     sun_direction = vec3(0.0, 1.0, 0.0);`, `uniform vec3 twilight_colour = vec3(0.0);`, `uniform
     float twilight_energy : hint_range(0, 16) = 0.3;`, `uniform vec3 skyglow = vec3(0.004, 0.005,
     0.009);`; a function before `sky()`, exactly:
     ```glsl
     vec3 twilight_and_glow(vec3 dir) {
     	vec2 eye_h = dir.xz;
     	vec2 sun_h = sun_direction.xz;
     	float facing = 0.0;
     	if (length(eye_h) > 1e-5 && length(sun_h) > 1e-5) {
     		facing = max(0.0, dot(normalize(eye_h), normalize(sun_h)));
     	}
     	float eye_elevation = degrees(asin(clamp(dir.y, -1.0, 1.0)));
     	float shape = (0.3 + 0.7 * pow(facing, 3.0)) * exp(-max(0.0, eye_elevation) / 12.0);
     	return twilight_colour * twilight_energy * shape + skyglow;
     }
     ```
     and `COLOR += twilight_and_glow(EYEDIR);` as the line just before each of Godot's two `COLOR *=
     exposure;` (the sun's branch and the night's). "Horizontal" and "elevation" use the shader's y as
     up, as Godot's own `UP` does.
   - the sun's disk (design's ruling of 2026-10-17): `uniform float sun_disk_deg : hint_range(0, 5) =
     0.53;` declared after `skyglow`, and Godot's three disk lines replaced, exactly:
     ```glsl
     float sunAngularDiameterCos = cos(radians(sun_disk_deg) * sun_disk_scale);
     float sunAngularDiameterCos2 = cos(radians(sun_disk_deg) * sun_disk_scale * 0.5);
     float sundisk = smoothstep(sunAngularDiameterCos, sunAngularDiameterCos2, dot(EYEDIR, normalize(sun_direction)));
     ```
     (Godot's read `LIGHT0_SIZE`, which is 0 with the light's angular distance 0, so no disk, and
     `cos_theta`). `LIGHT0_SIZE` then appears in no code line of the shader. The disk is drawn only in
     Godot's lit branch, so only while the sun light is shown (from −1°).
   - **`ground_color`'s declaration keeps Godot's default** (`uniform vec4 ground_color : source_color
     = vec4(0.1, 0.07, 0.034, 1.0);`): the rig sets the value (Decision 6), and the pin's census holds
     the declaration.
   The first line stays Godot's: `// NOTE: Shader automatically converted from Godot Engine
   4.6.3.stable's PhysicalSkyMaterial.` Godot's licence is in the credits already (MIT). The
   rehearsal's shader differs from Godot's generated code (Decision 9) in exactly seven `diff` hunks:

   physical sky draws the ground below the horizon as the horizon's light times `ground_color` (its
   line `Lin *= mix(ground_color.rgb, vec3(1.0), smoothstep(-0.1, 0.1, dot(UP, EYEDIR)));`), and the

   Godot's default ground (0.1, 0.07, 0.034) left a shaded vertical face at 0.24 of 0.3.1's light
   relative to lit flat ground (rehearsal, moon light off). The rule:
   - **ground_color = the village photo's mean linear colour × K**, K = (the irradiance of flat ground)
     / (π × the horizon's radiance) at the start's sun (2023-09-06 11:40 UTC, grid azimuth 184.65°,
     elevation 50.34°), per channel, measured in software Forward+ with the rig;

     shade, a cone like a tree, all roughness 0.95) the shaded face's displayed brightness over lit
     flat ground's, under the rig with the new ground, is at least the same ratio under 0.3.1's
     environment (main.tscn's procedural sky, exposure 0.54, sun energy 1), **both at the start's sun**;
     if K fails that floor, the smallest K that meets it, said so.
