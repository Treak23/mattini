## Goal

At night the sky shows the stars to magnitude 6 at their places for the time and the latitude, from a
table derived from the Hipparcos Catalogue that carries its own credit and the label of its changes.

- **The star table** `game/sky/stars.csv` (new), made by `tools/stars_table.py` (new) from the Hipparcos
  Catalogue's main file, which you download into `data/` and never commit: three credit lines, the column
  header and 5044 stars, byte for byte the pinned file.
- **The star image**: `SkyRig.star_image(path) -> Image` (in v0.4.0-05's `game/scripts/sky/sky_rig.gd`)
  draws the table into a 4096 × 2048 equirectangular image in celestial coordinates.
- **The celestial basis**: `SkyRig.apply` sets the sky shader's new `celestial_basis` from the frame, the
  latitude and the local sidereal time, and the shader (v0.4.0-05's `game/shaders/sky_physical.gdshader`)
  samples the night sky at the celestial coordinates of the view's direction instead of `SKY_COORDS`.

The rig is built and tested on its own nodes. Nothing the owner sees changes in this batch: the main
scene creates the rig with the star image from v0.4.0-08, which also shows the credit.

2. **The table's recipe** (the session's decision 2: M4-05 decisions 6 and 7 re-cut). Each record of
   `hip_main.dat` is one line of 450 characters and an LF, its fields separated by `|`. The fields used,
   1-based bytes from the ReadMe:
   ```
     18- 28  A11   ---     RAhms     Right ascension in h m s, ICRS (J1991.25) (H3)
     30- 40  A11   ---     DEdms     Declination in deg ' ", ICRS (J1991.25)   (H4)
     42- 46  F5.2  mag     Vmag      ? Magnitude in Johnson V                  (H5)
   ```
   - **every record whose Vmag is not blank and is 6.0 or less** (5044 records; one record has a blank V);
   - **positions from RAhms and DEdms for every star, one rule**: RAhms is `hh mm ss.ss` and DEdms
     `±dd mm ss.s` (the sign at byte 30); `ra = (h + m/60 + s/3600) * 15`, `dec = sign * (d + m/60 +
     s/3600)` in that order of operations, as floats. Every record carries H3 and H4, so no star is lost
     (RAdeg/DEdeg, fields H8 and H9, are blank for 263 records, three of them at V ≤ 6: xi UMa 3.79, xi
     Sco 4.16 and HIP 115125 5.19, which this rule keeps). **ICRS, epoch J1991.25, used as given: no
     precession, no proper motion** (Decision 10 says what that costs);
   - each star written `f"{ra:.4f},{dec:.4f},{v:.2f}"` (V as given: it has two decimals);
   - sorted by the written V, then right ascension, then declination, as numbers;
   - after the header lines of Decision 3; LF endings and a final LF.
   Expected: **5044 stars, 112'045 bytes, SHA-256
   `e2a98eea26d486805cc340e648f79235cedf6274a6d39d02712c908f72487d3f`**, the first star
   `101.2885,-16.7131,-1.44` (Sirius), with `279.2341,38.7830,0.03` (Vega) and `37.9462,89.2641,1.97`
   (Polaris). Design computed it twice, with two independent programs (Python slicing bytes; awk splitting
   on `|` with `sort`, `v0.4.0-06-stars-awk.sh`): the same bytes. The table's format and order are
   M4-05's; only the source, the header lines and the numbers changed.
3. **The header lines and the credit** (the session's decision 3, its words less "and colours": the
   table has no colours, Decision 9; the two further `# ` lines are the pin author's, accepted by the
   session 2026-10-27). The table begins with exactly these
   four lines (the first three start with `# `; the fourth is M4-05's column header):
   ```
   # Stars derived from the Hipparcos Catalogue (ESA 1997, ESA SP-1200): stars to V 6.0, positions rounded and reformatted. Credit: ESA. CC BY-NC 3.0 IGO, https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc/3.0/igo/
   # Changes made: from CDS I/239 hip_main.dat (53316318 bytes, SHA-256 58ceabb104d647160d9437ce6e513a02a036bb4ad9f8879a5a22fd52943616e0), the records with Vmag <= 6.0; right ascension and declination (ICRS, epoch J1991.25, not precessed) converted from h m s and d m s to degrees, rounded to 4 decimals; Vmag as given; all other fields dropped; sorted by V, right ascension, declination.
   # ESA's licence and credit for the catalogue: https://www.cosmos.esa.int/web/hipparcos/catalogues
   ra_deg,dec_deg,vmag
   ```
   Why the words matter: the table is an adaptation of the catalogue, and CC BY-NC 3.0 IGO lets an
   adaptation be shared only "provided that You clearly label, demarcate or otherwise identify that
   changes were made to the original Work" (§3(b)), with "a credit identifying the use of the Work in the
   Adaptation" and "the URI, if any, that the Licensor specifies to be associated with the Work"
   (§4(c)(iii), (iv)) and the licence's URI (§4(a)); a breach ends the licence (§7). The first line is
   the credit (its words, without `# `, are v0.4.0-08's `STARS_NOTE` in the game's credits; not yours);
   the second labels every change and names the exact source (lesson 41: a stored value names what it
   was computed from); the third is ESA's page. The pin compares all four lines exactly: never reword
   them.
4. **How you get the file, and the script refuses any other** (the session's decision 4). The download

5. **The star image** (M4-05 decision 8, unchanged). `static func star_image(path: String) -> Image` on
   `SkyRig`: 4096 × 2048, `FORMAT_RGBE9995` (Sirius at 3.8 and a 6th-magnitude star at 0.004 in one 4-byte
   texel; 32 MiB), no mipmaps; each star adds 10^(−0.4 V) to red, green and blue of row `min(floor((90 −
   dec) / 180 × 2048), 2047)`, columns `c − h … c + h` wrapped, `c = floor(ra / 360 × 4096) mod 4096`,
   `h = floor(0.5 / cos(dec))`. The run keeps a star about one texel high wide at any declination:
   measured by M4-05's design under Forward+, with one texel per star Polaris's texel is 0.001° wide and
   nothing shows; with the run it is the brightest pixel. **Lines starting with `# ` and the line
   `ra_deg,dec_deg,vmag` draw nothing**; any other line that is not three numbers is an error: an error
   line (`push_error`, naming the file and the line) and `null` (Hard Rule 2). The stars are white (the
   pin asserts Sirius's green and blue equal its red): see Decision 9. Rehearsal: the recipe lights 7204
   texels (7248 writes); the image's total flux 124.36464 against the table's 124.35632; 33 ms.
6. **`celestial_basis`, set by `apply`** (M4-05 decision 9, unchanged). The Basis turning a scene direction
   into celestial coordinates (x towards RA 0 on the equator, y towards RA 90°, z the north celestial
   pole). With the frame's east E, north N, up U (its columns), the latitude φ (`create`'s
   `latitude_deg`, kept by v0.4.0-05 for this) and the local sidereal time θ (`apply`'s `sidereal_deg`;
   rename v0.4.0-05's `_sidereal_deg`): pole P = N cos φ + U sin φ, the equator's meridian point Q = U cos
   φ − N sin φ, X = Q cos θ − E sin θ, Y = Q sin θ + E cos θ; the basis whose rows are X, Y, P
   (`Basis(X, Y, P).transposed()`), set every `apply` as the sky material's `celestial_basis`. M4-05's
   design measured under Forward+ with a coordinate image that the uniform reaches GLSL as GDScript
   multiplies it, neither mirrored nor transposed; the rehearsal measured it again (gate P2).
7. **The sky shader** (M4-05 decision 4, its section S bullet; every change marked `// mattini:` in a
   comment on its own line, as v0.4.0-05's):
   - one uniform after `stars_strength`: `uniform mat3 celestial_basis = mat3(1.0);`;
   - the night sky is sampled at the celestial coordinates of `EYEDIR`, never `SKY_COORDS`: `c =
     celestial_basis * EYEDIR`, `uv = (fract(atan(c.y, c.x) / TAU), acos(clamp(c.z, -1, 1)) / PI)` (u =
     right ascension / 360, v = (90° − declination) / 180°), in both of the night sky's lines. The
     rehearsal wrote a function before `sky()`:
     ```glsl
     vec2 celestial_uv(vec3 dir) {
     	vec3 c = celestial_basis * dir;
     	return vec2(fract(atan(c.y, c.x) / TAU), acos(clamp(c.z, -1.0, 1.0)) / PI);
     }
     ```
     and the two lines `L0 += texture(night_sky, celestial_uv(EYEDIR)).xyz * extinction *
     stars_strength;` and `COLOR = texture(night_sky, celestial_uv(EYEDIR)).xyz * stars_strength;`.
     v0.4.0-05's pin still holds them: it asks that the two night-sky lines start `L0 += texture(night_sky,`
     and `COLOR = texture(night_sky,` and contain `* stars_strength`, and that Godot's other lines stay in
     order. `SKY_COORDS` then appears in no code line (comments are not code). Nothing else in the shader
     changes; the header comment names this brief.
8. **Polaris and the pole** (M4-05 decision 10, unchanged). m4-design §6 pins Polaris at the latitude's
