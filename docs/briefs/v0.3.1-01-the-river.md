## Goal

The river in the village is drawn as the photo's own river, a little darker (the photo's colour
× 0.9 instead of × 0.6), and less like a mirror (roughness 0.3 instead of 0.05); its ripples come
from two noise normal maps tiling every 2 m and 3.4 m (0.3.0: 10 m and 17 m) that drift apart at
0.8 m/s each (0.3.0: about 0.25 m/s); its edge fades over 1 m at the bank instead of a 0.2 m
stepped line. All of it is one change to `game/shaders/terrain_photo.gdshader`: new water
uniforms, a new function `water_weight(xz)`, a new water rule. The gate's render step checks the
new water with M4-06's rewritten `classes` check (already pinned), and two text censuses pin the
shader's lines. Four software screenshots of a synthetic scene show the water before and after.

Production code is unchanged since 0.3.0 (`697a154`); M4-06 was rehearsed by design on that code
(2026-10-17), with the same water lines you write here, and this brief's pins and shader text were
rehearsed again by the pin author in a clone of `d724fb7` (2026-10-26): red, green, the full gate
and all eleven mutation rows. Every number below marked "rehearsal" was measured there.

## Decisions already made

1. **The water's uniforms and roughness** (M4-06 decision 4; replaces M3-10's `water_scale_m`,
   `water_drift_a`, `water_drift_b`, `water_darken`, strength 0.2 and roughness 0.05). The
   `group_uniforms water;` block and the constant become exactly:
   ```glsl
   group_uniforms water;
   // Two NoiseTexture2D normal maps (TerrainLoader makes them; no file). Map a tiles every
   // water_scale_a_m and drifts along water_drift, map b tiles every water_scale_b_m and drifts
   // against it: 0.8 m/s each way, no net direction (a torrent's turbulence; m4-design §8).
   uniform sampler2D water_normal_a : hint_normal, filter_linear_mipmap, repeat_enable;
   uniform sampler2D water_normal_b : hint_normal, filter_linear_mipmap, repeat_enable;
   uniform float water_scale_a_m = 2.0;
   uniform float water_scale_b_m = 3.4;
   uniform vec2 water_drift = vec2(0.48, 0.64); // m/s
   uniform float water_normal_strength = 0.35;
   // On water the photo's colour is multiplied by this: the photo's own river, a little darker.
   uniform float water_albedo = 0.9;
   group_uniforms;

   const float PHOTO_ROUGHNESS = 0.95;
   const float WATER_ROUGHNESS = 0.3;
   ```
   - The drift is one vector of 0.8 m/s, (0.48, 0.64) in local (x, z): map a's pattern moves
     along it, map b's against it. In the world's axes (x east, z south) a moves about 143°
     (south-east) and b about 323° (north-west). There is no single direction for the water as
     a whole: design's choice (M4-06 decision 4), kept by the owner at the go (below, 7).
   - The two water normal maps stay as `TerrainLoader` makes them today (256 px seamless simplex
     noise, bump strength 6.0, seeds 1 and 2): `terrain_loader.gd` is not touched.
2. **The water's weight, `water_weight(xz)`, the bank eased over 1 m** (M4-06 decision 4): the
   share of water among the 5 × 5 class texels (1 m at 0.2 m) around the point, each read with
   `texelFetch`; the boxes of the four nearest texel centres blended bilinearly, so 6 × 6 texels
   with the outer row and column weighted by the fraction (texel coordinates
   t = (xz − rect.xy) / rect.zw · size − 0.5, as `class_weights`); then `smoothstep(0, 1, share)`.
   On a straight bank the weight is 0.5 on the class edge, 0 from 0.5 m outside it, 1 from
   0.5 m inside; a channel narrower than 1 m never reaches full weight. Insert it **directly
   after `class_weights`' closing brace, before the comment `// The rock texture projected…`**,
   exactly:
   ```glsl

   // The water's weight at (x, z), its bank eased over 1 m (m4-design §8): the share of water among
   // the 5 x 5 class texels (1 m at 0.2 m) around the point, each read exactly (texelFetch); the
   // boxes of the four nearest texel centres blended bilinearly, so 6 x 6 texels with the outer row
   // and column weighted by the fraction; then a smoothstep. On a straight bank the weight is 0.5 on
   // the class edge, 0 from 0.5 m outside it and 1 from 0.5 m inside; a channel narrower than 1 m
   // never reaches 1. Texels are placed as in class_weights; outside the grid: no water.
   float water_weight(vec2 xz) {
   	ivec2 size = textureSize(surfaces, 0);
   	vec2 t = (xz - surfaces_rect.xy) / surfaces_rect.zw * vec2(size) - 0.5;
   	vec2 base = floor(t);
   	vec2 f = t - base;
   	ivec2 i0 = ivec2(base);
   	float share = 0.0;
   	for (int j = -2; j <= 3; j++) {
   		float wy = j == -2 ? 1.0 - f.y : (j == 3 ? f.y : 1.0);
   		for (int i = -2; i <= 3; i++) {
   			ivec2 p = i0 + ivec2(i, j);
   			if (p.x < 0 || p.y < 0 || p.x >= size.x || p.y >= size.y) {
   				continue;
   			}
   			float wx = i == -2 ? 1.0 - f.x : (i == 3 ? f.x : 1.0);
   			int c = int(texelFetch(surfaces, p, 0).r * 255.0 + 0.5);
   			share += wx * wy * float(c == 2);
   		}
   	}
   	return smoothstep(0.0, 1.0, share / 25.0);
   }
   ```
   (Indentation is tabs, as in the rest of the shader.) Why there: the pinned
   `test_m3_09_photo_shader.gd` requires water_weight's `vec2 t` line to come after
   `class_weights`' own, and requires everything from `// Varyings & Types` to
   `\t// The photo look.` to stay identical to the clay shader, so the function must sit above
   `// Varyings & Types`. Rock, road and path keep `class_weights` as they are (M3-10).
3. **The water rule in `fragment()`** replaces today's from `// Water: smooth, darker, its normals
   drifting.` to its closing brace, exactly:
   ```glsl
   	// Water: the photo's colour × water_albedo, roughness WATER_ROUGHNESS, two ripple maps
   	// drifting apart; its weight eased over 1 m at the bank.
   	float w_water = water_weight(xz);
   	if (w_water > 0.0) {
   		vec2 ua = (xz - TIME * water_drift) / water_scale_a_m;
   		vec2 ub = (xz + TIME * water_drift) / water_scale_b_m;
   		vec3 na = unpack_normal(textureGrad(water_normal_a, ua, ddx_xz / water_scale_a_m, ddy_xz / water_scale_a_m));
   		vec3 nb = unpack_normal(textureGrad(water_normal_b, ub, ddx_xz / water_scale_b_m, ddy_xz / water_scale_b_m));
   		vec2 ripple = (na.xy + nb.xy) * water_normal_strength;
   		bump = mix(bump, vec3(ripple.x, 0.0, ripple.y), w_water);
   		albedo *= mix(1.0, water_albedo, w_water);
   		roughness = mix(roughness, WATER_ROUGHNESS, w_water);
   	}
   ```
   `vec4 classes = class_weights(xz);` stays (rock, road and path read it); the water no longer
   reads `classes.y`. Water draws at every distance, as today.

## For the release's test list (the session writes it; nothing for the builder to do)

What the owner should look at on their PC, low over the Vispa in the village, looking upstream
(south, towards the sun) and downstream:
1. **Is the near-black patch near the camera gone?** It may not be the water's shading at all: a
   real-time shadow (the bridge's hump or the bank trees south of it, with the sun at 183°, 52°)
   or the photo's own baked shadow of the bridge (flown with the sun at about 185°, 50°, so it
   falls just north of the bridge, where the patch was) would stay dark: × 0.9 does not change
   either; only the lift (a later release) brightens baked shadows.
2. **Is the jagged white rim at the bank gone?** The 1 m ease widens the band of in-between
   roughness from 0.2 m to 1 m; looking towards the sun it may show as a broader bright edge
   (reasoned, not rendered on the village).
3. **Does the river read as moving?** Faster, smaller ripples, but no net downstream direction
   (the owner's choice, Decision 7).
4. **Do the ripples show beyond the first metres?** 2 m tiles flatten through their mipmaps
   closer to the camera than 0.3.0's 10 m tiles did.
5. **Looking towards the sun:** a patch of glints where the highlight meets the river is by
   design (roughness 0.3), not a fault.

   water_weight's 36 class reads on every terrain fragment, water or not (never measured on a
