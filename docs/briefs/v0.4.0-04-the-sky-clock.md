## Goal

The game knows where the sun and the moon stand over Zermatt at any moment from 2020 to 2035, what
the clocks in Zermatt read then, and the local sidereal time the stars need.

- **`SkyClock`** (`game/scripts/sky/sky_clock.gd`, new): static, pure, headless. It takes a time in
  Unix seconds UTC and a place, and returns the sun's and the moon's azimuth and elevation, the moon's
  illuminated fraction, the local mean sidereal time, Zurich's UTC offset and the text of the in-game
  clock.
- It holds no state and reads no clock, file or setting. Nothing calls it yet: the main scene
  (v0.4.0-08) will own the running time and take the place from `config/sky.json` through the
  manifest (v0.4.0-07); the clock label (v0.4.0-09) will show `local_text`. Nothing the owner sees
  changes in this batch.

1. **The class** (M4-04 decision 1, verbatim). `class_name SkyClock`, `extends RefCounted`, every
   function `static`:
   - `const START_UNIX := 1694000400.0`: 2023-09-06T11:40:00Z, 13:40 CEST, the photo's moment
     (SPEC §13 row 43).
   - `zurich_offset_s(unix: float) -> int`: 7200 from 01:00 UTC on the last Sunday of March up to
     (not including) 01:00 UTC on the last Sunday of October of the year of `unix` (UTC), else 3600.
     A rule, not a table (SR 941.299.1, the EU rule): one function, so a change of the rule is one
     edit. A fractional time counts as the second it falls in.
   - `local_text(unix: float) -> String`: `"6 Sep 2023 13:40 CEST"`: the day without a leading zero,
     the English three-letter month (`Jan` … `Dec`), the year, hours and minutes with two digits each
     (24-hour, `01:59`), `CET` or `CEST`. Minutes are truncated, never rounded: 13:40:59.9 reads
     13:40.
   - `sun(unix: float, lat_deg: float, lon_deg: float) -> Vector2`: (azimuth, elevation) in degrees;
     azimuth true, clockwise from north, in [0, 360); elevation apparent: the true elevation plus
     NOAA's refraction, exactly as the spreadsheet: none above 85°; 58.1/tan e − 0.07/tan³ e +
     0.000086/tan⁵ e above 5°; 1735 − 518.2 e + 103.4 e² − 12.79 e³ + 0.711 e⁴ above −0.575°;
     −20.772/tan e below (arcseconds). The longitude is east-positive. UTC stands in for UT1 (under
     0.9 s).
   - `moon(unix: float, lat_deg: float, lon_deg: float) -> Vector3`: (azimuth, elevation,
     illuminated fraction): Schlyter's elements of the moon and the sun, Kepler's equation solved to
     convergence, the perturbations in longitude (12 terms), latitude (5) and distance (2), the
     ecliptic of date to the equator, then the topocentric correction (parallax asin(1/r), geocentric
     latitude and ρ as Schlyter's §13) against the hour angle from `sidereal_deg`. No refraction. The
     fraction is (1 − cos elongation) / 2, the elongation from Schlyter's sun and the moon's ecliptic
     longitude and latitude. Our own code; Schlyter's page named in a comment.
   - `sidereal_deg(unix: float, lon_deg: float) -> float`: Meeus 12.4, 280.46061837 +
     360.98564736629 (JD − 2451545) + 0.000387933 T² − T³ / 38710000, plus the east longitude, in
     [0, 360). JD = unix / 86400 + 2440587.5.
2. **Precision** (M4-04 decision 2). GDScript `float` is 64-bit; `Vector2` and `Vector3` are 32-bit,
   which keeps an azimuth to about 1e-5°: compute in `float`, build the vector last.
3. **The tolerances** (M4-04 decision 3; m4-design §5), in the pinned tests: the sun 0.05° where the
   expected elevation is above 2°, 0.3° at or below (design's addition: below −5° too, where §5
   names none); the moon 0.5° in azimuth × cos(elevation) and in elevation, the fraction 0.03;
   sidereal 0.01°; the offset and the text exact.
4. **The vectors** (M4-04 decision 4; `tests/shared/sky_vectors.json`, design data, 295 instants
   from 2020 to 2035 at 46.0200 N, 7.7490 E, 1610 m, plus 12 at three other places): the sun from
   pvlib's SPA, its expected elevation SPA's true elevation plus NOAA's refraction (SPA refracts
   nothing below −0.83°, where NOAA adds up to 0.47°: more than the 0.3° band, so a correct NOAA
   clock would fail SPA's own apparent elevation between −1.1° and −0.83°; SPA's own value is kept in
   the file for reference); the moon from skyfield with JPL's DE421 (topocentric, no refraction); the
   sidereal time from skyfield at UT1 = UTC; the offset and the text from Python's zoneinfo. The tests
   pass the vectors' own site to the clock. The site is within 1e-3° of `config/sky.json`'s
   (46.0196233 N, 7.7485397 E; re-checked 2026-10-27: 0.00038° and 0.00046°), so nothing is
   substituted.
5. **Measured** (M4-04 decision 5, re-measured by the rehearsal, 2026-10-27): a clock written from

   tolerance and the moon 0.304 (GUT prints both lines: `sun: worst error …`, `moon: worst error …`);

## Appendix: the methods, written out (angles in degrees unless said; all in 64-bit `float`)

(The pin author's write-up of M4-04's three sources, as formulas; accepted by the session

JD = unix / 86400 + 2440587.5. Trigonometry on degrees: sin x means sin(deg_to_rad(x)); atan2 and
asin results converted back with rad_to_deg. "mod" is `fposmod`.

**The sun (NOAA's spreadsheet; UTC, time zone 0).** T = (JD − 2451545) / 36525.
- L0 = mod(280.46646 + T (36000.76983 + 0.0003032 T), 360); M = 357.52911 + T (35999.05029 −
  0.0001537 T); e = 0.016708634 − T (0.000042037 + 0.0000001267 T).
- C = sin M (1.914602 − T (0.004817 + 0.000014 T)) + sin 2M (0.019993 − 0.000101 T) + sin 3M ·
  0.000289.
- Ω = 125.04 − 1934.136 T; λ (apparent longitude) = L0 + C − 0.00569 − 0.00478 sin Ω.
- ε0 = 23 + (26 + (21.448 − T (46.815 + T (0.00059 − 0.001813 T))) / 60) / 60; ε = ε0 + 0.00256 cos Ω.
- δ (declination) = asin(sin ε sin λ).
- y = tan²(ε / 2); equation of time (minutes) = 4 · rad_to_deg(y sin 2L0 − 2 e sin M + 4 e y sin M
  cos 2L0 − 0.5 y² sin 4L0 − 1.25 e² sin 2M) (the bracket is an angle in radians; rad_to_deg makes
  it degrees, and the sun moves one degree in 4 minutes).
- minutes = mod(unix, 86400) / 60; true solar time TST = mod(minutes + equation of time + 4 · lon,
  1440); hour angle H = TST / 4 − 180.
- cos z = sin φ sin δ + cos φ cos δ cos H (φ the latitude; clamp to [−1, 1]); z = acos; true elevation
  e = 90 − z.
- a = acos(clamp((sin φ cos z − sin δ) / (cos φ sin z), −1, 1)); azimuth = mod(a + 180, 360) where
  H > 0, else mod(540 − a, 360).
- Apparent elevation = e + refraction(e) / 3600 with Decision 1's refraction (arcseconds), chosen on
  the true elevation e.

**The moon (Schlyter).** d = JD − 2451543.5 (Schlyter's day 0, 1999-12-31 00:00 UT).
- The sun (§4): w_s = 282.9404 + 4.70935e-5 d; e_s = 0.016709 − 1.151e-9 d; M_s = mod(356.0470 +
  0.9856002585 d, 360); the ecliptic's obliquity ecl = 23.4393 − 3.563e-7 d. E_s by Kepler (below);
  v_s = atan2(√(1 − e_s²) sin E_s, cos E_s − e_s); the sun's longitude λ_s = v_s + w_s.
- The moon (§4): N = 125.1228 − 0.0529538083 d; i = 5.1454; w = 318.0634 + 0.1643573223 d; a =
  60.2666 (Earth radii); e = 0.054900; M = mod(115.3654 + 13.0649929509 d, 360).
- Kepler: E0 = M + rad_to_deg(e sin M (1 + e cos M)); repeat E ← E − (E − rad_to_deg(e sin E) − M) /
  (1 − e cos E) until the step is below 1e-9° (a bound such as 50 rounds; it converges in a few).
- xv = a (cos E − e); yv = a √(1 − e²) sin E; v = atan2(yv, xv); r = √(xv² + yv²).
- xh = r (cos N cos(v + w) − sin N sin(v + w) cos i); yh = r (sin N cos(v + w) + cos N sin(v + w) cos
  i); zh = r sin(v + w) sin i; λ = atan2(yh, xh); β = atan2(zh, √(xh² + yh²)).
- §9: Mm = M; Ls = M_s + w_s; Lm = M + w + N; D = Lm − Ls; F = Lm − N. Then λ += −1.274 sin(Mm − 2D)
  + 0.658 sin 2D − 0.186 sin M_s − 0.059 sin(2Mm − 2D) − 0.057 sin(Mm − 2D + M_s) + 0.053 sin(Mm + 2D)
  + 0.046 sin(2D − M_s) + 0.041 sin(Mm − M_s) − 0.035 sin D − 0.031 sin(Mm + M_s) − 0.015 sin(2F −
  2D) + 0.011 sin(Mm − 4D); β += −0.173 sin(F − 2D) − 0.055 sin(Mm − F − 2D) − 0.046 sin(Mm + F −
  2D) + 0.033 sin(F + 2D) + 0.017 sin(2Mm + F); r += −0.58 cos(Mm − 2D) − 0.46 cos 2D.
- To the equator: xg = r cos λ cos β; yg = r sin λ cos β; zg = r sin β; ye = yg cos ecl − zg sin
  ecl; ze = yg sin ecl + zg cos ecl; RA = atan2(ye, xg); Dec = atan2(ze, √(xg² + ye²)).
- §13, topocentric: LMST = `sidereal_deg(unix, lon)`; mpar = asin(1 / r); gclat = φ − 0.1924 sin
  2φ; ρ = 0.99833 + 0.00167 cos 2φ; HA = LMST − RA; topRA = RA − mpar ρ cos gclat sin HA / cos Dec;
  g = atan(tan gclat / cos HA) and topDec = Dec − mpar ρ sin gclat sin(g − Dec) / sin g; where gclat
  is 0 (the equator) instead topDec = Dec − mpar ρ sin(−Dec) cos HA (Schlyter's own case).
- To the horizon (HA' = LMST − topRA): x = cos HA' cos topDec; y = sin HA' cos topDec; z = sin
  topDec; xhor = x sin φ − z cos φ; zhor = x cos φ + z sin φ; azimuth = mod(atan2(y, xhor) + 180,
  360); elevation = asin(clamp(zhor, −1, 1)).
- The fraction: cos elongation = cos(λ_s − λ) cos β (λ and β after the perturbations); fraction =
  (1 − cos elongation) / 2.

**The sidereal time (Meeus 12.4).** D = JD − 2451545; T = D / 36525; θ0 = 280.46061837 +
360.98564736629 D + 0.000387933 T² − T³ / 38710000; LMST = mod(θ0 + lon, 360).

**The offset and the text.** s = `floori(unix)`; the UTC year of s; spring = the last Sunday of March
00:00 UTC + 3600 s, autumn = the last Sunday of October 00:00 UTC + 3600 s (seconds since 1970);
offset = 7200 where spring ≤ s < autumn, else 3600. The text: local = s + offset; days = floor(local /
86400); the second of the day = local − days · 86400; the date of `days`; hours = second / 3600 and
minutes = (second mod 3600) / 60, both integer division (truncated); `"%d %s %d %02d:%02d %s"` with
day, month name, year, hours, minutes, `CEST` for 7200 else `CET`.
