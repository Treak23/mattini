## What you get

0.3.1's village with:

- **A river that flows downstream.** Every stream's ripples drift along its own course, from swissTLM3D's watercourse lines
  (the catalogue says they point downstream; all 56 stretches in the village box measured downhill): a 2 m flow grid the surfaces
  step paints, read by the game through the world config, both ripple patterns drifting along it at 0.8 and 0.5 m/s in two
  crossfaded phases of 2 s (so nothing jumps when the clock wraps: 0.3.1's hourly jump is gone).
- **The sun and the moon move.** They stand where they stand over Zermatt at the game's time, starting at the photo's moment
  (6 September 2023, 13:40 CEST). Keys: `,` and `.` go 15 minutes back and forward, `[` and `]` a day, `T` steps the speed
  (1×, 60×, 600×, stopped), `N` sets the computer's time; a clock label shows the time and the speed for 4 seconds after each
  key and while the pause screen is open. The river's ripples keep real time whatever the clock does.
- **A physical sky:** twilight, the sun's disk, exposure 0.70 by day and 4.0 at night, the moon by its phase and the stars
  (5'044, from the Hipparcos Catalogue, credited to ESA on the pause screen). The sky's ground below the horizon is the
  valley's own colour (your "3a"), so shaded walls and tree sides keep 0.3.1's light.
- **Small things:** a pipeline file error prints one line (no traceback); the release checklist keeps the 60 fps and
  `override.cfg` checks; the clerk's inbox commits take the hygiene tests (words); the sky's error messages in a release say
  to download the release folder again.

Everything else is 0.3.1's: the controls, the village, the credits' swisstopo lines, MSAA 4×, 60 fps (15 paused).

### The trees: what the renders show (software Forward+, 13:40, MSAA off; hand-picked boxes, so numbers to compare, not to trust)

|---|---|---|---|---|
| E1 the start view (the sun in front: the backlit side) | 14.5; 80.5 % near-black | 16.6; 75.0 % | 38.1; 0.1 % | 15.9; 77.4 % |

What they say: the forest is near-black in the shade mostly because little light reaches it, not because its colour is dark:
brightening the crowns lifts them only 2 to 3 levels, a brighter sky ground 1 to 2. A light haze (the sky's colour added with
distance: it changes everything far away, not only the trees; its density is a guess, 10 % at 2 km if Godot's fog behaves as
assumed, not measured) lifts distant forest to a dark grey-blue and removes the near-black share. With the sun behind the
camera (E2) the trees already read as dark green and brightening them lifts them to 50 to 73. Pictures:
`docs/briefs/shots/v0.4.0-release-e1-*.jpg`, `-e3-*.jpg`.

## What the review found

Each batch was built by a builder agent on Sonnet in its own worktree (its pinned tests red first, then green, its mutation
rows run) and reviewed by its tier: tier 1 (01, 02, 03, 04, 07, 08) by three lens reviewers, a mutation prover and, where it
found gaps, a fixer and a read-only verifier; tier 2 by one reviewer. The provers found real gaps in six of the tier-1
batches (the flow PNG's bound and two guards of the flow's description; the flow's Hard Rule 4 twin, tolerance, digest case
and height; eleven guards of the sky step; the sky wiring: the stars turning, the moon's phase, the credit shown only when
stars are drawn, a manifest-side type guard): each closed by a new test and verified; accepted as equivalent or below the
tolerance: the moon's smaller terms, the refraction cutoff, scene values the rig overwrites, a finiteness check that JSON
cannot trigger. The whole-range review (four lenses over `891f49a..4f7304e`, two skeptics per P0 to P2 finding;
`docs/reviews/v0.4.0-round1.md`) found **no P0 and no P1**; three P2s were graded P3 by their skeptics. In plain words, the P3s
(the board has them all):

- nothing but a words check at the release gate proves the star table is in the export (a code check is next release's);
- the placeholder sun (used only when no sky block was written) is a hand copy of a config value;
- the day keys can take the clock outside the 2020 to 2035 range the sun and moon are validated for, with no clamp or label
  (Questions, 2);
- two time keys read "Period" on the pause screen under the render container's keyboard map (probably its layout; look at it);
- a one-texel seam in the night sky at right ascension 0°, the sky shader assuming "up" is +y, `png_l8` messages saying
  "classes" for the flow, an azimuth that can round to 360.0 once in 25 million instants.

Process notes, for the ledger: one first run was BLOCKED (batch 06: the catalogue's host reset every connection; answered by a
commit that lets the builder copy the session's verified file); every tier-1 batch whose prover found unpinned guards needed a
test-only round 2 because every test file in its Scope was a pinned copy (the next release's briefs should allow one new test
file per tier-1 batch); one reviewer's finding (an empty Findings table) was stale because it read only the feature commit.

   pulse from the crossfade; streaks where streams meet (the flow turns within one 2 m cell); the speeds (0.8 and 0.5 m/s) are
   look values; water more than 12 m from every drawn stream line stands still.
3. **The day, by the keys:** dawn, noon, dusk and night; the sky's colours, the twilight, the exposure (0.70 by day);
   the moon by its phase; the stars (positions are J1991.25: 0.13 to 0.35° from the date's, not corrected; white, no colours)
   and ESA's credit on the pause screen.
4. **The sun leaves the village later than the real one:** the far mountains are not modelled yet, so on the start date the
   village keeps the sun about 8 minutes early and 24 minutes late, up to an hour on winter evenings (the next release but one).
5. **The sky's ground** beyond the village's edge reads about twice the terrain's brightness, and there is a yellow-green band at
   the horizon (the measured costs of the floor you chose in "3a").
6. **The pause screen at your window size:** the Controls list clears Resume by 9.5 px at 1280 × 720 in the software render; the
   two time keys' names; the clock label's size and place.
