## What you get

0.1.0's village terrain, now with its buildings: all 1'486 buildings of the 2 km × 3 km
village box, from swissBUILDINGS3D 3.0, edition 2020 (© swisstopo), the source you chose.
They look like a white architectural model: matte off-white walls, roofs a shade darker
and warmer, flat facets, shadows in the fixed sun. There are no textures and no colours
per building, because the data has none and nothing is invented. The two materials,
`game/materials/building_walls.tres` and `building_roofs.tres`, are yours to tune in the
editor.

The controls, the pause screen and the start view are 0.1.0's. The credits now carry
`swissbuildings3d 2020, © swisstopo` and godot-cpp's MIT notice, the one 0.1.0 lacked, so
this build may be shared.

**To start it:** as 0.1.0, the three files of your platform together in one folder.
Windows: `windows/mattini.exe`, `mattini.pck`, `libterrain.windows.release.x86_64.dll`;
double-click `mattini.exe` (unsigned: More info, then Run anyway). Linux:
`linux/mattini.x86_64`, `mattini.pck`, `libterrain.linux.release.x86_64.so`; `chmod +x
mattini.x86_64`, then `./mattini.x86_64`. The executables and libraries are the same files
as 0.1.0's; only `mattini.pck` changed.

## What M2 built

- **M1's review residue** (M2-01 `b5a45e5`, M2-02 `2cf5660`, M2-06 `cefa1ac`). Every built
  terrain is compared with its designed SHA-256 before it is written. The tests build from
  2 × 2 tiles and read a second fixture north-west of its origin. The manifest checks the
  terrain's source, box, rows and columns. The clay look is tested, and the export starts
  the Linux build and requires its startup line.
- **The building inputs** (M2-03 `ad12280`): the LK25 map-sheet grid, and `lock buildings`
  and `download buildings`, with the terrain's guarantees.
- **The village buildings** (M2-04 `b980214`): `python -m mattini buildings` turns the six
  sheets into six 1 km chunks. That is 1'486 buildings, 92'594 vertices and 178'910
  triangles, the same bytes on every machine; each chunk's SHA-256 is design data,
  computed two independent ways.
- **The buildings in the game** (M2-05 `86d764c`). The chunks are read and checked against
  their SHA-256 and the world config, then drawn after the terrain. A refused building set
  opens the error panel while the terrain stays.
- **The review's fixes** (M2-07 `b3ec242`, M2-08 `20c5a80`). Expected values name the world
  and inputs they were computed for, so a changed world gets a refusal that says so. The
  buildings are proven to stand where the world config puts them, and their look is
  tested. With no terrain, the camera starts over the buildings. The gate and the export
  are stricter.
- **The real sample** (M2-09 `eced376`, your "yes"): swisstopo's sheet 1328-33 (45'668
  bytes, three buildings) is the buildings reader's test file, the one raw file in git.

## What the review found

Round R1: three Opus reviewers (placement at Tier 1, inputs, game and tools), and each
finding of grade P2 or above was put to three skeptics. There was no P0 and no P1; five P2
and sixteen P3, weaknesses that nothing trips today. The code itself was measured right: a
third independent derivation of the six chunks matched byte for byte, 17 buildings placed
by hand through the world config landed where the game puts them, and the ground lies
between floor and roof for 1'475 of the 1'486 buildings. In plain words:

- After a changed world config or an adopted republished tile, the refusal blamed "this
  machine or this code" and named a command that could not help (P-02, I-01). Fixed: the
  expected values record what they were computed for, and the refusal names design's step.
- Nothing tested that the buildings stand where the world config puts them in the scene,
  and the test world's box lay 4 m under its own ground (P-01, P-04). Fixed and tested.
- The buildings' look had no test (G-01), and without terrain the camera started 1.6 km
  below the village (G-02). Both fixed.
- Smaller items: the download's guarantees before a fetch, two tracebacks, the sheet grid's
  edge, stale chunk files, the export script's order, the gate's GUT version and
  allowlist. All fixed.

A verification pass over the fixes found every one fixed, and seventeen new P3s, all
weaknesses in tests or messages that need a hand-edited file or a future change to matter.
One was fixed at this close; sixteen are M3's first work. The real-sample test's own check
found that design's test missed a turned triangle; fixed at this close.

## What 0.2.0 does NOT prove: please look at these

1. **That it renders and looks right on your RTX 4090.** There is no GPU here. Look for
   white buildings standing on the clay terrain (not floating, not sunk), roofs a shade
   darker than the walls, shadows, and no holes or inside-out faces.
2. **The buildings the data puts partly under or above the ground.** The terrain is 2024;
   the buildings were surveyed mostly in 2012. Twelve stand above the terrain at a corner,
   by up to 4.1 m, two of them marked "under construction". Eight have their roof below the
   2024 ground at their centre, so they show barely or not at all; one of them is
   swisstopo's own "invisible" class. Both lists are in SPEC §8 with their ids. If one
   looks wrong, a screenshot to `feedback` says which.
3. **That the Windows build starts,** and that the Linux build starts on EndeavourOS.
4. **Memory and frame rate on a GPU:** 875 MiB was measured headless, which is not the
   GPU's load.
5. **How the white model reads** from above and at street level. The colours are yours to
   react to.

## If it does not start

1. Keep the three files together, with their names unchanged.
2. Find the log: Windows `%APPDATA%\Godot\app_userdata\mattini\logs\godot.log`, Linux
   `~/.local/share/godot/app_userdata/mattini/logs/godot.log`.
3. Roll back: `dist`'s `mattini-0.1.0/` holds 0.1.0, the terrain without the buildings.
   Send the log and a screenshot to `feedback`.
