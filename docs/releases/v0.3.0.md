## What you get

0.2.0's village, with the white model replaced by a look made from swisstopo's data:

- **The photo.** SWISSIMAGE 2023 lies on the whole 2 km × 3 km box at 0.2 m (10'000 × 15'000 texels; the
  photo's real detail is 25 cm), as its own texture over the terrain. It was flown on 6–7 September 2023, at
  about 13:30 local time, so its shadows are baked in and point north.
- **Roads, paths, water and rock** from swissTLM3D (edition 2026-02) are drawn on the ground with free CC0 detail
  textures from Poly Haven (a rock face, worn asphalt, a rocky trail; their licence notice ships with them).
  The water moves. Streams are drawn 2 m wide, a labelled placeholder.
- **Trees.** 34'412 generic conifers stand where the LiDAR surface model (swissSURFACE3D) shows trees, after the
  ones on rock and inside building footprints were taken out. One kind only: a placeholder, said so in the credits.
- **Roofs.** Each of the 1'486 roofs has its own colour, measured from the photo.
- **Walls.** One neutral plaster for every wall, a labelled placeholder: there is no open wall data (your choice,
  "neutral").
- **Light.** The sun stands where it stood in the photo (azimuth 183°, 52° above the horizon), and the
  exposure and sky are set so that flat sunlit ground renders at the photo's own brightness (exposure 0.54,
  sky 0.25). That was calibrated in a software render, not on Forward+.
- **Data packs.** The data is now in three files per platform beside the game, each under GitHub's 100 MiB
  limit: `mattini.pck`, `mattini-terrain.pck` and `mattini-photo.pck`. Keep all five files of a folder together
  (the game, the three packs and Terrain3D's library). Without a pack the game opens its error panel and says what
  to do: download the folder again.

The controls, the pause screen and the start view are 0.2.0's. The credits now list five datasets, once each
(swissALTI3D 2024, swissBUILDINGS3D 2020, SWISSIMAGE 2023, swissSURFACE3D 2021–2022, swissTLM3D 2026-02, all
© swisstopo), the three textures and the placeholders.

**To start it:** as 0.2.0, with the packs beside the game. Windows: double-click `mattini.exe` (unsigned: More
info, then Run anyway). Linux: `chmod +x mattini.x86_64`, then `./mattini.x86_64`. The executables and Terrain3D's
libraries are byte for byte 0.2.0's; only the `.pck` files changed.

## What the review found

Round R1 had three reviewers (placement at Tier 1, inputs, game and tools), each building the real village from the
pinned inputs: 0 P0, one P1, eight P2 and thirteen P3. Round V1 checked the fixes made by then, round R2 all six.
In plain words:

- **The trees were lit from the wrong side** (the one P1): their faces' normals pointed into the tree, in both
  renderers, so every conifer would have looked dark. Fixed and tested (fix-4).
- **Trees on a softer terrain.** At the 1 m or 2 m terrain that §7 offers as a config change, thousands of the
  34'412 trees would have floated or sunk, with every command at exit 0. The trees step and the manifest now refuse any
  spacing but 0.5 m and say so (fix-2; your answer, row 37). On the real village at 0.5 m none of the trees is more than
  0.3 mm off Terrain3D's ground.
- **Nothing in the gate ran the shaders that place the photo, the classes and the crowns' colour.** Six placements of them,
  such as the photo upside down, left the whole gate green. The gate now has a render step (a software render of a test
  world, under Xvfb) that measures where they land; every one of those placements fails it (fix-1, fix-3; your answer, row 38).
- **A wrong-typed manifest field** could make a guard accept a block it should refuse (a numeric `world_sha256` passed all
  six readers). The readers now refuse a field of the wrong type, with tests that fail without the guard (fix-5).
- **The surfaces PNG's bytes depended on the machine's zlib**, and so did `manifest.json`. On a machine whose zlib writes other
  bytes (zlib-ng) the step now refuses, naming the zlib and the fix, and writes nothing; the manifest compares it too
  (fix-6; row 39). Round R2 broke the refusal 26 ways and 23 failed a named test; the three that did not are P3.
- **Round R2 found no P2 or above** in any lens. Its seven P3 and the older ones are on the board's residue list.

## What 0.3.0 does NOT prove: please look at these

1. **That it renders and looks right on your RTX 4090.** There is no GPU here. Every image seen so far is a software render
   (Compatibility renderer), not Forward+, and not your colour.
2. **The photo on the terrain:** its sharpness at 30, 60 and 150 m.
3. **The baked shadows.** The photo was taken at about 13:30 in September, so its shadows point north, and the game's sun is
   placed there (azimuth 183°). Under any other sun the photo's shadows would still point north (the moving sun is M4).
4. **The "ghost roofs":** buildings newer than the 2020 model have no building in the game but their roof is in the photo,
   so some roofs lie flat on the ground. The newer data is one 14 GB national file; not in M3.
5. **Roads, paths, water and rock.** Do the roads and paths read on the ground? Does the water move? Is the rock face's
   grain right on steep rock? Below about 30 m grass and scree are soft photo pixels (you chose not to add close-range detail).
6. **The trees:** generic conifers where the LiDAR found them. In software renders they read near black; Forward+ is the check.
7. **The walls:** one placeholder colour.
8. **The roof colours,** measured from the photo for 1'486 of 1'486 roofs.
9. **The light:** exposure 0.54 and sky 0.25 were calibrated in software, not on Forward+; it may read lighter or darker for you.
10. **Memory and GPU memory.** Headless, the exported build peaks at 1'607 MiB resident. The photo alone is expected to take
    0.6–0.8 GB of GPU memory, about 2–3 GB in all; that number is yours to read on your card.
11. **The Windows build, never started here.** The Linux build was started headless only; it was not shown on a screen.
12. **What the new render step does not see** (round R2, P3): it reads the water class only, not rock, road or path; it
    would pass with no tree drawn at all, and its line "over 20 trees" counts the trees in its test world, not the ones
    drawn; its test world is flat, 16 m wide and has only positive coordinates, so it says nothing of distance, of the
    terrain's mesh or of a sign error that shows at negative coordinates. Those stay with fix-1's text checks and with you.
13. **Windows' zlib was not measured.** The surfaces step refuses on a machine whose zlib writes other bytes; on 0.3.0's
    pinned Python (below 3.14) the village's PNG was measured byte-identical on zlib 1.2.11, 1.2.13, 1.3, 1.3.1 and
    1.3.2, and different on zlib-ng.
14. **The game on a 1 m or 2 m terrain,** by design: the trees step refuses it.

## Not in 0.3.0 (said out loud, from the plan)

Close-range detail for meadows, scree and forest floor (you chose to let the photo carry them); façade materials beyond one
plaster; tree species, broad-leaved trees and bushes; railways, cable cars and names; lightening the photo's baked shadows
(needs the moving sun, M4); buildings newer than 2020; the area beyond the 2 × 3 km box (M5); delivery beyond packs on
`dist` (M5's size decides).

## If it will not start

1. Check that all five files of the platform's folder are together with their names unchanged: the game, `mattini.pck`,
   `mattini-terrain.pck`, `mattini-photo.pck` and Terrain3D's library. The error panel names the missing pack and the fix
   (download the folder again).
2. Godot writes a log on every start: Windows `%APPDATA%\Godot\app_userdata\mattini\logs\godot.log`, Linux
   `~/.local/share/godot/app_userdata/mattini/logs/godot.log`. Send it and a screenshot to the `feedback` session.
3. The rollback is `mattini-0.2.0/` on `dist` (the village with the white model), with its three files.
