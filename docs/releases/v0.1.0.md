## What you get

The village of Zermatt as terrain: the 2 km × 3 km box around the village at 0.5 m
(swissALTI3D 2024, © swisstopo), in a clay relief-model look with thin contour lines every
10 m and stronger ones every 100 m, lit by one fixed sun from the south-south-east. You fly
over it freely: hold the right mouse button to look; W and S fly along the view, A and D
sideways, E and Q straight up and down; Shift is five times faster; the mouse wheel sets
the speed (2 to 1000 m/s, starting at 30). Escape opens the pause screen: the version (the
only place it shows), the controls, the credits, Resume and Quit.

Only the village box is drawn. There are no buildings yet (M2) and no mountains around it,
the Matterhorn included (M5): the start view faces the Matterhorn's direction, and past
the terrain's edge you see the sky's neutral grey ground.

**To start it:** take the three files of your platform from `dist` and keep them together
in one folder. Windows: `windows/mattini.exe`, `mattini.pck`,
`libterrain.windows.release.x86_64.dll`; double-click `mattini.exe`. The file is not
signed, so Windows may say "Windows protected your PC": More info, then Run anyway.
Linux: `linux/mattini.x86_64`, `mattini.pck`, `libterrain.linux.release.x86_64.so`;
`chmod +x mattini.x86_64`, then `./mattini.x86_64`. Loading the village takes about 5
seconds after the window opens.

## What M1 built

- **The tile lock.** `config/datasets.lock.json` pins the SHA-256 of the six village
  tiles; `download terrain` refuses a tile swisstopo republished before it fetches or
  deletes anything. The manifest and `provenance.json` are pinned to exact bytes. (M1-01,
  `25972d4`)
- **A gate that cannot be switched off.** Four ways to cut the test run short or turn its
  checks off now fail it; `WorldConfig` is tested in all 24 axis rotations. (M1-02,
  `c635411`)
- **`python -m mattini terrain`.** The six tiles become one 4001 × 6001 heights file at
  0.5 m, the same bits on every machine (`b6334c1f…`). (M1-03, `0794554`)
- **Terrain3D 1.0.2 in the game.** Every height on its own vertex; the clay look; software
  screenshots. (M1-04, `2160a3c`)
- **The main scene.** The free-fly camera, the pause screen, credits from the manifest,
  and errors on screen that name the command that fixes them. (M1-05, `29e829f`)
- **`tools/export.sh`.** One command builds the Linux and Windows exports and checks every
  file against GitHub's limit. (M1-06, `5c421ca`)

## What the review found

Round R1: two Opus reviewers, terrain at Tier 1 and the game at Tier 2. No P0 and no P1;
8 P2 and 10 P3, weaknesses that nothing trips today. The code itself was measured right:
the village sits exactly on its vertices at 0.5, 1 and 2 m, and a real export loads it. In
plain words:

- The tests' data is tidier than the village. The pipeline is tested on one whole tile,
  the game on one small grid at 0.5 m; a mistake that swapped the village's northern and
  southern kilometres, or moved it 2 km north, would pass every test (T-01, T-02).
- Nothing compared the finished village with its designed SHA-256, and nothing started
  the exported build. Both were done by hand at this close; M2 automates both (T-01,
  G-02).
- The clay look has no test: a checkerboard or a black village would pass (G-01).
- A date or a host name could slip into the manifest, and `manifest` does not notice a
  tile the lock no longer pins (T-03, T-04).
- The check for a second copy of the origin misses three spellings (T-05).
- Smaller items: an allowlist entry wider than its reason, a comment that claims more than
  its code, stale texts (G-03 to G-08, T-06 to T-10).

Fixed at this close: G-08 (the release checklist) and the SPEC half of T-08. Declined:
T-09 and T-10, each guarded elsewhere. The other fifteen are the first work of M2, before
any M2 code that would trip them.

## What 0.1.0 does NOT prove: please look at these

1. **That it renders and looks right on your RTX 4090.** The containers have no GPU; the
   screenshots in `docs/briefs/shots/` are software renders in the Compatibility renderer,
   evidence of what is drawn, not of the look. Look for: a light warm-grey clay surface
   (not a checkerboard, not black), the thin 10 m and stronger 100 m contour lines,
   shadows from the sun in the south-south-east, the sky.
2. **That the Windows build starts at all.** Nothing ran on Windows.
3. **That the Linux build starts on EndeavourOS**, with another glibc and driver than the
   container's.
4. **How the controls feel:** the speeds, the mouse (0.25° per pixel), the 1.5 m floor
   above the ground.
5. **That the pause screen reads well at your screen size**, and that the credits scroll.
6. **That your PC's pipeline builds the same village bytes**, if you run the pipeline
   yourself (the build already carries the checked file).

## If it does not start

1. Keep the three files of your platform together in one folder, their names unchanged.
2. Godot writes a log on every start: Windows
   `%APPDATA%\Godot\app_userdata\mattini\logs\godot.log`, Linux
   `~/.local/share/godot/app_userdata/mattini/logs/godot.log`.
3. Send the log and a screenshot to `feedback`. There is no earlier version to roll back
   to: 0.1.0 is the first.

## Before you share it

The credits lack the MIT notice of godot-cpp, a library compiled into Terrain3D's (review
G-06). M2 adds it. Until then 0.1.0 is best kept to your own PCs.

## Good to know

140 MB for this release, more with each. To work on the code without it:
`git clone --single-branch --branch main https://github.com/Treak23/mattini`.
